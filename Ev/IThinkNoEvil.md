**The False Addiction**

People said I was addicted to smoking. They were wrong. The cigarettes were never the point.

Smoke gave shape to empty hours. It justified standing outside. It filled the silence between conversations. It made waiting feel like doing something. The addiction was not nicotine. It was ritual. It was an excuse to pause. To belong. To make ordinary moments seem deliberate.

Others saw dependence. I saw habit dressed as necessity.

---

**Smoking to Death**

There was a time I wondered how long a person could keep smoking before the body finally refused.

Not because I wanted nicotine. Because I wanted proof. Proof that I could disappear slowly, one cigarette at a time, and no one would notice until the ending had already begun.

It was a quiet kind of suicide. Not dramatic. Not immediate. Just a bargain with time.

The bargain never lasted.

---

**Three Months**

One day I stopped.

No ceremony. No patches. No promises.

Three months passed without a cigarette.

The cravings came and went like weather. They knocked on the door, found no answer, and eventually wandered elsewhere. That was when I understood something.

If addiction ruled me, I could not have left it behind so easily.

Willpower was always stronger than the smoke.

---

**Sugar**

The cigarettes disappeared.

The sugar remained.

Candy wrappers gathered in drawers. Bags of chips vanished between meals. Chocolate bars became breakfast. Cookies became rewards for surviving ordinary days.

A two-liter bottle of soda emptied with unsettling speed. The fizz was familiar. Comfortable. Easier than water.

This addiction looked sweeter.

---

**Health**

People assumed I did not care.

They were only half right.

I exercised. I moved. I kept telling myself that as long as I stayed active, everything would balance itself. Every workout became permission for another bottle, another handful of candy, another late-night snack.

Health became an equation I kept rewriting.

One side always outweighed the other.

---

**The Warnings**

My parents warned me about diabetes.

Friends repeated the same stories. High blood sugar. Needles. Blindness. Limbs. Regret.

Every warning arrived carrying someone else's fear.

For a while I believed them.

Then the fear faded, just like every headline and every promise to eat better. The candy remained. The soda remained.

The warnings were louder than the habit, but never stronger.

---

**Control**

People like clean definitions.

Either addicted or not.

I never fit comfortably into either.

I smoked until I stopped.

I drank soda until I decided not to.

I could quit for weeks. Months. Long enough to prove a point, then return because I chose to, not because I had forgotten the lesson.

Perhaps that is the cruelest illusion.

Control does not always mean freedom.

Sometimes it only means choosing the length of the leash.
